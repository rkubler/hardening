E6 · Models designed for ratcheting

Jiang–Sehitoglu (1996)

The recall of each back stress grows as a power χ of its magnitude, without the Macaulay bracket of Ohno–Wang. χ = 0 gives Armstrong–Frederick; large χ approaches a critical-state model. Each run compares Chaboche, Jiang–Sehitoglu and Ohno–Wang II with m = χ.

Constitutive equations

dXi = ci ri [ n̄ − (‖Xi‖/ri)χi+1 Li ] dq    Li = Xi/‖Xi‖,   dq = ‖dεp‖ = √(3/2) dp
  • n̄ is the unit normal (flow direction); ‖·‖ is the Euclidean norm of the tensor, and ri is the limit of ‖Xi‖.
  • The parameters are entered here as Chaboche Ci, γi, so that the loop shape is the same as in Chaboche: ri = √(2/3) Ci/γi and ci = Ci/(1.5 ri). The uniaxial back stress then saturates at Ci/γi.
  • χ = 0: the recall is c √(3/2) X dq = γ X dp, i.e. Armstrong–Frederick exactly. Large χ: almost no recall below ri, a critical-state behaviour like Ohno–Wang I.
  • In the original model χi can depend on the non-proportionality of the loading and on the stress level; here it is a constant, the same for all terms.

What to look for

Ratcheting. Increasing χ reduces the ratcheting rate continuously from the Chaboche value. In uniaxial loading, Jiang–Sehitoglu with exponent χ and Ohno–Wang II with m = χ have the same recall while the flow is along X; they differ just after each reversal, where Ohno–Wang II has no recall (Macaulay bracket) and Jiang–Sehitoglu still has some. That is why their curves are close but not identical.

Axial stress + cyclic shear. Without the bracket, the recall acts whatever the angle between the flow and the back stress, so Jiang–Sehitoglu ratchets more than Ohno–Wang II in this test. Compare the drift of the yield surface for the two models in the panel below.
