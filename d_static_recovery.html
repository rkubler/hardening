D · Time-dependent kinematic hardening

Static recovery (time-dependent)

An Armstrong–Frederick back stress that also decays with time, even without plastic flow. During a hold at constant strain the back stress recovers and the stress relaxes; during a rest at zero stress the material softens. Each run is compared with the same model without recovery.

Constitutive equations

dX = 2⁄3 C dεp − γ X dp − bs J(X)rs − 1 X dt      f = J(σ − X) − σy ≤ 0
  • The first two terms are the Armstrong–Frederick law (dynamic recovery, driven by plastic strain).
  • The last term is static recovery: it acts with time, also when the material is elastic. bs (MPa1−rs/s) sets its speed and rs its sensitivity to the back stress level. It represents thermally activated rearrangement of dislocations.
  • Here plastic flow is rate independent; in practice static recovery is combined with a viscoplastic flow rule, which adds creep and rate effects.

What to look for

Hold at constant strain. The back stress decays, so σ − X grows and reaches the yield surface: plastic flow occurs at constant total strain and the stress relaxes. Without recovery the stress stays constant.

Rest at zero stress. After unloading, the back stress keeps decaying. On reloading the material yields at a lower stress: the recovery has erased part of the prior hardening.

Dwell cycles. Each hold at a strain peak relaxes the stress, and the back stress is partly erased. The next half-cycle then starts from a smaller back stress, which changes the shape of the loop: the peak stress itself can even end slightly higher, while the stress at the end of each dwell is much lower. The same mechanism, acting on a mean stress, causes the thermal relaxation of residual stresses at high temperature.
