A · Nonlinear kinematic hardening

Armstrong–Frederick model (1966)

One back stress with a hardening term and a recall term, plus optional Voce isotropic hardening. Run a monotonic test, strain-controlled cycles or stress-controlled cycles, and watch the back stress saturate, the Bauschinger effect appear and the strain ratchet under a mean stress.

Constitutive equations

dX = 2⁄3 C dεp − γ X dp      f = J(σ − X) − (σy + R) ≤ 0      R = Q (1 − e−b p)
  • Hardening term ⅔ C dεp: pushes the back stress along the plastic flow. C is the initial slope (MPa).
  • Recall term −γ X dp: pulls the back stress back towards zero, in proportion to X and to the accumulated plastic strain dp. γ sets how fast X saturates at C/γ.
  • Voce term R: the elastic domain grows from σy to σy + Q. Set Q = 0 for pure kinematic hardening.

What to look for

Monotonic test. The uniaxial back stress follows X = (C/γ)(1 − e−γ εp) and reaches 95 % of its saturation at εp ≈ 3/γ.

Strain cycles. Reverse yielding starts long before −σmax (Bauschinger effect). With a mean strain, the mean stress relaxes towards zero, because the recall term acts in both directions.

Stress cycles. With a non-zero mean stress and a stress range larger than 2(σy + Q), the loops do not close and the strain ratchets at a constant rate per cycle. Once R has saturated, the rate is δεp = (1/γ) ln[((C/γ)² − (σmin + k)²) / ((C/γ)² − (σmax − k)²)] with k = σy + Q. If the range is smaller, the elastic domain grows until it covers the cycle and the strain stops (shakedown). A single Armstrong–Frederick term strongly overestimates ratcheting compared with real materials.
