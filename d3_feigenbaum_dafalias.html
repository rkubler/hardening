D3 · Microstructure-based and distortional models

Feigenbaum–Dafalias (2007): directional distortional hardening

Kinematic and isotropic hardening, plus a distortion of the yield surface linked to the back stress direction. The surface becomes egg-shaped: it stretches and sharpens in the loading direction and flattens on the opposite side, as measured in yield surface probing experiments. The tests run on a thin-walled tube in tension–torsion; watch the shape in the yield surface display.

Constitutive equations

f = (s − α) : [ H0 + (nr : α) A ] : (s − α) − k² = 0,   nr = (s − α) / |s − α|
dα = (C n − γ α) dp      dA = −c [ (nr : α) nr ⊗ nr + x A ] dp      dk = b (k0 + Q − k) dp
  • H0 gives the von Mises circle; A (a fourth-order tensor, zero initially) distorts it. The distortion is multiplied by nr : α, which is positive on the side the back stress points to (the front) and negative on the opposite side (the back): the same A stretches the front and flattens the back.
  • α follows Armstrong–Frederick (saturation C/γ) along the flow direction n, the normal to the distorted surface; k is the size, with Voce isotropic hardening.
  • A grows with c and saturates at A = −(nr : α)/x nr ⊗ nr. In the loading direction the term (nr : α)(nr : A : nr) reaches −|α|²/x: the surface is longer in front by the factor 1/√(1 − |α|²/x) and shorter at the back by 1/√(1 + |α|²/x).
  • Because the distortion depends on the back stress, it disappears when α returns to zero and it switches sides when α reverses, which keeps the model simple. The surface must stay convex, which limits |α|²/x; this page asks for (C/γ)²/x ≤ 0.8.

What to look for

Yield surface. After tension, play the history in the yield surface display: the circle becomes an egg pointing in the loading direction, with a flat back. The flow arrow, normal to the distorted surface, is no longer along the line from the centre to the stress point.

Reverse loading. The flat back brings reverse yielding earlier than with kinematic hardening alone (stronger Bauschinger effect). During the reverse flow, α and A change sign and the egg turns round.

Orthogonal change. In shear just after tension, the stress meets the side of the egg, where the distortion is small, so the re-yielding is set mainly by the translated circle; the shape then rotates towards the shear direction.

The equations are written here in the (σ, √3 τ) plane, where H0 becomes the identity and A a 2 × 2 symmetric matrix. As in the 2007 paper, the evolution of A is proportional to the plastic multiplier; the factor ‖s − α‖² of the original rule is absorbed into c here. Feigenbaum and Dafalias (2008) also proposed a simpler version with a second-order tensor. Parameters are illustrative.
