Burlet–Cailletaud (1986): radial evanescence

Only the part of the back stress along the flow direction is recalled. In uniaxial loading this is exactly Armstrong–Frederick; under multiaxial loading it strongly reduces ratcheting. Each run compares both rules in a tension–torsion ratcheting test or a uniaxial test.

Constitutive equations

Burlet–Cailletaud:   dX = 2⁄3 C dεp − γ (X : n̄) n̄ dp
Armstrong–Frederick:   dX = 2⁄3 C dεp − γ X dp      n̄ = √(2/3) n,   ‖n̄‖ = 1
  • n̄ is the unit normal to the yield surface, i.e. the direction of plastic flow.
  • (X : n̄) n̄ is the projection of the back stress on that direction: the radial evanescence. The part of X perpendicular to the flow is not recalled.
  • In uniaxial loading X is always parallel to the flow, so the projection is X itself and both rules coincide.

What to look for

Axial stress + cyclic shear. The axial stress creates an axial component of the back stress. With Armstrong–Frederick, every shear cycle recalls it, the yield surface drifts and the tube elongates at a constant rate. With Burlet–Cailletaud the flow is mostly in shear, the axial component lies almost perpendicular to it and is hardly recalled: axial ratcheting stops after a few cycles. Real materials usually lie in between, which motivated Delobelle's combination (E2).

Uniaxial stress cycles. The two curves are identical: switching to Burlet–Cailletaud does nothing against uniaxial ratcheting.
