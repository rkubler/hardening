E7 · Models designed for ratcheting

Chen–Jiao–Kim (2005)

Ohno–Wang model II with an exponent χ on the Macaulay bracket. In uniaxial loading it is identical to Ohno–Wang II; under non-proportional loading χ < 1 strengthens the recall and increases multiaxial ratcheting. Each run compares Chaboche, Ohno–Wang II (χ = 1) and your χ.

Constitutive equations

dXi = γi [ 2⁄3 ri dεp − (X̄i/ri)mi Xi ⟨dεp : ki⟩χ dp1−χ ]    = γi [ 2⁄3 ri dεp − (X̄i/ri)mi ⟨n : ki⟩χ Xi dp ]
  • n : ki measures the alignment between the plastic flow and the back stress Xi: 1 when they are parallel, 0 when perpendicular.
  • χ = 1 gives Ohno–Wang II. In uniaxial loading n : k = ±1, so ⟨n : k⟩χ is 1 or 0 whatever χ: the model is identical to Ohno–Wang II.
  • χ < 1 raises ⟨n : k⟩χ when 0 < n : k < 1, i.e. under non-proportional loading, so the recall is stronger there. Chen, Jiao and Kim introduced it because Ohno–Wang II underestimated multiaxial ratcheting of a medium carbon steel.

What to look for

Axial stress + cyclic shear (default). Lowering χ below 1 increases axial ratcheting compared with Ohno–Wang II, while Chaboche stays far above both. χ is identified on a multiaxial ratcheting test, after m on uniaxial tests.

Uniaxial tests. The Chen–Jiao–Kim and Ohno–Wang II curves coincide exactly: χ has no effect in uniaxial loading.

Use the yield surface panel to see the angle between the flow arrow and the back stress direction (dotted line from the origin to ×): it is this angle that χ acts on.
