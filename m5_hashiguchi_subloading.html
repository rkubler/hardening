M5 · Multisurface and two-surface models

Hashiguchi subloading surface model (1989)

There is no purely elastic domain. The stress always lies on a subloading surface, similar to the normal-yield surface and smaller by the ratio R. Plastic strain develops as soon as R grows, slowly when R is small and faster as R approaches 1. The result is a smooth elastic–plastic transition and plastic strain even in cycles below the yield stress. Three versions are compared.

Constitutive equations (uniaxial form)

|σ − ᾱ| = R F   (subloading surface)      |σ − α| ≤ F   (normal-yield surface)      ᾱ = s − R (s − α)
dR = U dp,   U = −u ln R      dα = (C ν − γ α) dp      ds = dα + c (σc − s) dp,   σc = α + ν F
  • Normal-yield surface of size F, centred on α, with Armstrong–Frederick kinematic hardening (C, γ). It plays the role of the usual yield surface.
  • Similarity centre s: the subloading surface is the image of the normal-yield surface in a homothety of centre s and ratio R, so its centre is ᾱ = s − R(s − α). The stress is always on it.
  • Normal-yield ratio R (0 ≤ R ≤ 1): R = 0 when σ = s, R = 1 when the stress is on the normal-yield surface. During loading R grows with dR = U dp; the function U = −u ln R is infinite at R = 0 and zero at R = 1, so R tends to 1 smoothly. u sets how quickly the curve reaches the normal-yield surface.
  • Loading criterion: plastic flow occurs whenever R increases; when R decreases the response is elastic. There is no elastic domain of finite size.
  • c: moves the similarity centre towards the conjugate point σc on the normal-yield surface, so that it follows the loading. With c = 0, s moves with α only.
  • Plastic modulus: differentiating σ = s + R (σc − s) gives dσ/dp = ds/dp + U (σc − s) + R (dα/dp − ds/dp). The first two terms make the response very stiff when R is small.

What to look for

Monotonic test. The subloading curves leave the elastic line gradually, instead of with a sharp yield point at F. All curves meet at large strain, when R ≈ 1, where the model behaves like Armstrong–Frederick.

Stress cycles below the yield stress. With σmax < F the conventional Armstrong–Frederick model stays elastic. The subloading model gives open loops and a slow ratcheting strain, as many metals show in high-cycle tests. Moving the similarity centre (c > 0) reduces the accumulated strain.

Stress cycles above the yield stress. The subloading model ratchets more than the conventional model, because plastic strain also develops in the part of each cycle where the conventional model is elastic. In the original model this ratcheting is often too large; later extensions of the subloading model were proposed to reduce it.

Strain cycles. The loops are rounded at both ends and R oscillates below 1 after each reversal.

This is a uniaxial version of the model, with the similarity centre rule written as ds = dα + c (σc − s) dp. Hashiguchi’s full multiaxial formulation also includes isotropic hardening of F, the norm of the plastic strain rate and a constraint that keeps s inside the normal-yield surface; the equations above keep its structure in uniaxial loading without isotropic hardening.
