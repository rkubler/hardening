D2 · Microstructure-based and distortional models

Barlat homogeneous anisotropic hardening, HAH (2011)

There is no back stress. Instead, the yield surface is distorted: it is flattened on the side opposite to the last loading direction, which is remembered by a microstructure deviator ĥ. The surface centre stays at the origin and its size follows the monotonic hardening curve. The model describes the Bauschinger effect, the transient after a reversal and the permanent softening, without kinematic hardening.

  • φ is a stable, isotropic or anisotropic yield function; here von Mises. ε̄ is the equivalent plastic strain (by plastic work).
  • ĥ, the microstructure deviator, is set to the stress direction at first yielding. It follows the loading direction at the rate k, but it does not flip after a reversal (cos χ = −1 gives dĥ = 0).
  • f1 acts only on the side where ĥ : s < 0 (the reverse side), f2 only on the forward side. With gk = 1, fk = 0 and Φ = φ. In uniaxial loading the yield stress on the side controlled by gk is simply gk σ̄.
  • k2, k3: during forward loading g1 decreases to k3 σ̄(0)/σ̄, so the reverse side flattens (Bauschinger effect). k1: after reversal g1 recovers quickly (transient).
  • k4, k5: g1 recovers only to g4 → k4 < 1, so the reverse curve remains below the monotonic one (permanent softening). g3 plays the same role for the next reversal.

What to look for

Reverse loading. The yield surface display shows the flattened back of the surface. After reversal, the curve rises steeply back towards the monotonic curve (g1 recovers at the rate k1) and stays slightly below it (k4). Compare with D1, where the back stress moves the surface instead.

Orthogonal change. The original 2011 model does nothing special here: ĥ : s = 0, the fluctuating terms vanish and the material re-yields on the monotonic curve. Then ĥ rotates towards the shear direction at the rate k. Later versions of HAH (from 2013) added terms for cross loading (latent hardening and the related softening), which are not included here.

Strain cycles. The forward and reverse sides take turns being flattened, so the loops have a smooth Bauschinger shape and the peak stress follows the monotonic curve as ε̄ accumulates.

The model is written here in the (σ, √3 τ) plane, where von Mises is a circle and ĥ is a unit direction; with the normalisation ĥ : ĥ = 3/8, the fluctuating terms reduce to fk J ⟨∓cos χ⟩. Parameters are illustrative, of the order of those reported for steel sheets.
