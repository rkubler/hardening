EVP4 · Elastoviscoplastic (rate-dependent) models

Bodner–Partom (1975)

A unified model without yield surface: the plastic strain rate is an exponential function of the ratio between a hardening variable Z and the stress. Plastic flow exists at every stress but becomes negligible at low stress, which gives a smooth, almost elastic start. Z grows with the plastic work.

Constitutive equations

ε̇p = (2/√3) D0 exp[ −n + 1⁄2n (Z / |σ|)2n ] sign(σ)
Ż = m (Z1 − Z) Ẇp,   Ẇp = σ ε̇p,   Z(0) = Z0
  • The uniaxial form comes from D2p = D02 exp[−((n + 1)/n)(Z²/3J2)n], the original equation written with the second invariants of the plastic strain rate and of the stress.
  • D0 (1/s) is the limit strain rate, reached at very high stress (often 104 /s). n controls the rate sensitivity: a small n gives a strong rate dependence, a large n an almost rate-independent response.
  • Z is the internal variable (a resistance to plastic flow, in MPa). It grows from Z0 to the saturation Z1 at a rate set by m (1/MPa) and by the plastic work: this is isotropic hardening.
  • There is no yield function and no loading–unloading test: the same equation is used everywhere, which makes the model simple to integrate. Later versions added a directional (kinematic) part of Z and thermal recovery; they are not included here.

What to look for

Tension at several rates. The curves leave the elastic line smoothly, without a yield point. The flow stress grows slowly with the rate (logarithmic-like), then sharply near D0; try n = 0.5 and n = 3.

Creep. Classical primary creep: the creep rate falls by several decades as Z grows with the plastic work. With Z1 finite the creep rate tends to a very small value; the model has no steady creep without a recovery term.

Relaxation. The stress relaxes quickly at first, then almost stops: with the exponential law, a small decrease of stress divides the rate by a large factor.

Strain cycles. Z only grows, so the loops harden cycle after cycle; and since Z is isotropic, reverse yielding happens at the same stress magnitude as forward yielding: no Bauschinger effect, unlike EVP3.

Parameters are illustrative, of the order of those reported for titanium and nickel alloys at moderate temperature.
