Constitutive equations

X = Σi Xi      dXi = 2⁄3 Ci dεp − γi Xi dp      f = J(σ − X) − (σy + R) ≤ 0
  • Each term is an Armstrong–Frederick law. Term i saturates at ri = Ci/γi, after a plastic strain of about 3/γi.
  • In uniaxial tension: σ = σy + R + Σi (Ci/γi)(1 − e−γi εp).
  • γi = 0 gives a linear (Prager) term; Ci = 0 switches the term off.

What to look for

With γ values separated by decades, the term with the large γ (about 10³) saturates within a few tenths of a percent and gives the sharp transition after yield. The intermediate term forms the knee of the curve. The term with the small γ (1 to 10, or 0) hardens almost linearly, keeps a long memory and controls ratcheting and mean stress relaxation.

In strain cycles, the fast terms switch sign at each reversal while the slow term barely moves: the loop shape comes from the fast terms, the long-term drift from the slow one.

Under stress cycles with a mean stress, the strain still ratchets at a constant rate per cycle, because every term has the Armstrong–Frederick recall. The threshold model (C), and the models in E, were designed to reduce this.
