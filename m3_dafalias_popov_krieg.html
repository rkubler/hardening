M3 · Multisurface and two-surface models

Dafalias–Popov (1975) and Krieg (1975) two-surface model

A yield surface moves inside a bounding surface. The plastic modulus depends on the distance δ between the stress and the bounding surface: very stiff just after yielding, it decreases smoothly to the bounding modulus when the two surfaces touch. Two ways of normalising δ are compared: the Dafalias–Popov rule, reset at each reversal, and a fixed reference in the spirit of Krieg.

Constitutive equations (uniaxial form)

|σ − α| ≤ k   (yield surface)      |σB − β| = K   (bounding surface)      dσ = H dεp,   H = H0 + h δ / (δin − δ)
  • Yield surface of size k, centred on α. In uniaxial loading its centre simply follows the stress: α = σ − k during tensile flow.
  • Bounding surface of size K > k, centred on β. It hardens linearly with the bounding modulus H0: dβ = H0 dεp. The stress can never cross it.
  • Distance δ = |σB − σ|, where σB = β ± K is the image point on the bounding surface in the direction of loading.
  • Plastic modulus: H tends to H0 when δ → 0, and is infinite when δ = δin. The shape parameter h sets how fast the curve approaches the bound.
  • Dafalias–Popov: δin is the value of δ at the start of each plastic loading in a new direction, so every reverse branch starts with an infinite modulus and a smooth elastic–plastic transition.
  • Krieg-type variant: δin = 2(K − k), fixed, so H depends only on the current distance δ. Krieg (1975) used a limit surface and a loading surface with a plastic modulus interpolated from the distance between them; this fixed-reference form is a simple uniaxial version of that idea, not his exact equations.

What to look for

Monotonic test and strain cycles. Both versions give a smooth elastic–plastic transition with very few parameters, and the cyclic curves are close to measured ones. A mean strain gives a mean stress that relaxes slowly.

Partial unloading and reloading. This is the known weakness of the Dafalias–Popov rule. After a small reverse plastic excursion, δin is reset to a small value. On reloading the model is again very stiff and the reloading curve overshoots the curve it would have followed without the excursion (positive values in the third plot). With a fixed reference, the modulus is the same function of δ before and after, so there is no overshoot. Later two-surface models keep a memory of the earlier loading to avoid it.

Stress cycles. Both versions ratchet under a mean stress. The amount depends strongly on h and on the δin rule, which is why ratcheting was later improved with nonlinear kinematic rules for the bounding surface.

The original model is multiaxial: σB is found with the radial (Mróz-type) mapping rule and the yield surface translates along σB − σ. In uniaxial loading these rules reduce to the ones above.
