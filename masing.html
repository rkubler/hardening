What to look for

Masing's rule states that a loop branch, measured from its reversal point, is the cyclic stress–strain curve σ = f(ε) scaled by a factor of two: σ − σr = 2 f((ε − εr)/2). In practice, one stabilized loop is then enough to predict the loops at every other amplitude.

For the non-Masing material, each branch still ends on the dashed curve, because its tip is a point of the cyclic curve, but the paths differ: the elastic part at the start of the branch gets longer as the amplitude grows. This is typical of 316L stainless steel. Such materials need a memory of the strain range in the model.

Both materials are illustrative. The Masing material follows a Ramberg–Osgood cyclic curve (E = 200 GPa, K′ = 1100 MPa, n′ = 0.15). The non-Masing material adds an elastic range that grows with the amplitude (about 76, 112 and 160 MPa).
