Ohno–Wang (1993), models I and II

Three back stresses whose recall only switches on near a critical value ri = Ci/γi, and only while the flow has a component along the back stress. Each run compares Chaboche, Ohno–Wang I and Ohno–Wang II with the same Ci and γi.

Constitutive equations

dXi = γi [ 2⁄3 ri dεp − wi ⟨dεp : ki⟩ Xi ]     ri = Ci/γi,   X̄i = J(Xi),   ki = Xi/X̄i
Model I:   wi = H(X̄i − ri)      Model II:   wi = (X̄i/ri)mi
  • The hardening term γi ri = Ci is the same as in Chaboche, so the loop shapes are almost the same.
  • Recall weight wi: Model I has no recall at all below the critical value ri (H is the Heaviside step). Model II has a weak recall that grows as (X̄i/ri)m; the larger m, the closer to Model I.
  • Macaulay bracket ⟨dεp : ki⟩: the recall only acts while the plastic flow has a component along Xi, not just after a reversal.

What to look for

Ratcheting. The tiles above the plots give the final strain and the rate of the last cycle of each model. Chaboche ratchets at a constant rate. Ohno–Wang I stops ratcheting quickly (often too quickly compared with experiments). Ohno–Wang II keeps ratcheting at a slow, decreasing rate, which is usually closest to measurements; increase m to slow it further.

Mean stress relaxation. Chaboche relaxes the mean stress to zero within a few tens of cycles. Ohno–Wang II relaxes it slowly and continuously, roughly as N−1/m: this is the behaviour often observed for residual stresses in cyclic tests.

Axial stress + cyclic shear. The Macaulay bracket ⟨dεp : ki⟩ also reduces multiaxial ratcheting: in shear, the flow is nearly perpendicular to the axial part of the back stresses, so their recall is small. Use the yield surface display to follow how the surface drifts along the σ axis in each cycle.

Ohno–Wang I does not relax at all here: at these small plastic strains no back stress reaches its critical value ri, so there is no recall. This is why Model I underestimates relaxation and Model II is preferred for residual stresses.
