EVP2 · Elastoviscoplastic (rate-dependent) models

Bingham (1922) and Perzyna (1966): overstress models

Viscoplasticity with an elastic domain. Below the yield stress the material is elastic and does not creep; above it, the plastic strain rate grows with the overstress, the distance between the stress and the yield stress. Bingham uses a linear law, Perzyna a power law. Each run compares both with the rate-independent limit.

Constitutive equations

σy(p) = σy0 + H p      overstress ⟨|σ| − σy(p)⟩
Bingham:   ṗ = ⟨|σ| − σy⟩ / η      Perzyna:   ṗ = ε̇0 ⟨(|σ| − σy) / σy⟩n      ε̇p = ṗ sign(σ)
  • ⟨x⟩ = max(x, 0): inside the elastic domain (|σ| ≤ σy) there is no plastic flow at all, whatever the time.
  • Bingham (originally for viscoplastic fluids such as paints and muds): the overstress is proportional to the plastic strain rate, with the viscosity η (MPa·s).
  • Perzyna (1966) generalised it to f(σ)/σy raised to a power n, which is the form used in most finite element codes. To compare like with like, ε̇0 is computed here so that both laws give the same overstress at the reference rate ε̇ref: ε̇0 = ε̇ref / (η ε̇ref/σy0)n.
  • Rate-independent limit: η → 0 (here η = 10−3 MPa·s). The stress stays on the yield surface |σ| = σy(p): this is classical plasticity with linear isotropic hardening H.
  • In tension at a constant rate the flow stress tends to σy + η ε̇ (Bingham) or σy [1 + (ε̇/ε̇0)1/n] (Perzyna).

What to look for

Tension at several rates. The curves start at the same elastic line and rise above the rate-independent curve by the overstress. In the second plot, Bingham's flow stress grows linearly with the rate (exponentially in the log axis): its rate sensitivity is far too strong over several decades. Perzyna's grows as a power: the two agree only near ε̇ref.

Creep. The default stresses are below σy0, just above it, and well above it. Below σy0 nothing happens: a threshold model does not creep below its yield stress, unlike Norton (EVP1). Above it the material creeps, and since σy grows with p, the creep stops when σy0 + H p reaches the applied stress. Set H = 0 to get unbounded steady creep.

Relaxation. The stress relaxes towards the yield stress σy(p), not to zero: the overstress disappears and the elastic domain remains. Bingham relaxes exponentially, with the time constant η/(E + H); Perzyna more slowly at the end, because its rate falls as the overstress to the power n.

Strain cycles. Faster cycling gives larger loops. With isotropic hardening only there is no Bauschinger effect; for that, a back stress is needed (EVP3).

Parameters are illustrative. The rate-independent model is the limit of both laws for η → 0 or ε̇0 → ∞, which is how many codes regularise rate-independent plasticity.
