E5 · Models designed for ratcheting

Abdel-Karim–Ohno (2000)

A combination of Ohno–Wang model I and Armstrong–Frederick, tuned by one parameter μ. A weak, permanent recall (μ) keeps ratcheting going at a steady, small rate instead of stopping as with Ohno–Wang I. Each run compares μ = 1 (Chaboche), μ = 0 (Ohno–Wang I) and your μ.

Constitutive equations

dXi = γi [ 2⁄3 ri dεp − μi Xi dp − H(fi) ⟨dλi⟩ Xi ]
fi = X̄i² − ri²,   dλi = dεp : ki − μi dp,   ri = Ci/γi,   X̄i = J(Xi),   ki = Xi/X̄i
  • μ X dp: a weak Armstrong–Frederick recall that always acts, even below the critical value ri.
  • H(f) ⟨dλ⟩ X: the Ohno–Wang I critical-state term. It only acts when X̄i reaches ri, and keeps the back stress on the critical surface while the flow pushes outwards.
  • μ = 0 gives Ohno–Wang I exactly; μ = 1 gives Armstrong–Frederick (Chaboche) exactly. Typical values are 0.01 to 0.2, here the same for all terms.

What to look for

Ratcheting. Ohno–Wang I quickly reaches shakedown and stops ratcheting, which often underestimates experiments. Chaboche ratchets far too fast. With a small μ the ratcheting rate settles to a small, almost constant value: μ is the parameter that sets the steady ratcheting rate.

Mean stress relaxation. Ohno–Wang I does not relax at small amplitudes, because no term reaches its critical value. The μ term relaxes the mean stress, roughly exponentially with the number of cycles, at a rate proportional to μ: a small μ gives slow relaxation.

Axial stress + cyclic shear. Watch the yield surface: with μ the surface keeps drifting slowly along σ, while with Ohno–Wang I it stabilizes.
