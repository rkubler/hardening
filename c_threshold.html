Threshold model (Chaboche, 1991)

A three-term Chaboche model where the recall of the last back stress only acts above a threshold X̄l. Each run compares it with the same model without threshold, under stress cycles (ratcheting) or strain cycles with a mean strain (mean stress relaxation).

Constitutive equations

dXi = 2⁄3 Ci dεp − γi Xi dp   (i = 1, 2)
dX3 = 2⁄3 C3 dεp − γ3 ⟨J(X3) − X̄l⟩ X3/J(X3) dp
  • ⟨x⟩ = max(x, 0) is the Macaulay bracket. While J(X3) < X̄l, the third term has no recall and hardens linearly, like Prager.
  • X3/J(X3) is the unit direction of X3: above the threshold, the recall grows as J(X3) − X̄l.
  • X̄l must be below C3/γ3, otherwise the recall never acts. X̄l = 0 gives the standard Chaboche model.

What to look for

Ratcheting. Without threshold, the strain grows at a constant rate per cycle. With the threshold, the norm of X3 stays below X̄l (second plot), so its recall never acts: the third term hardens linearly, like Prager. After a few cycles the loops close and ratcheting stops (shakedown). If you lower X̄l below the level that J(X3) reaches, the recall switches on and ratcheting continues, more slowly.

Mean stress relaxation. Without threshold the mean stress relaxes to zero. With the threshold it stops at a plateau whose level is set by X̄l: this is the model to choose when residual stresses stabilize at a non-zero value.
