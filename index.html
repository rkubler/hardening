Hardening simulators

Interactive simulators of the cyclic hardening of metals, written for teaching. Elastoviscoplastic models, phenomenological and physics-based, are also given to describe the rate dependence of plastic flow: creep, relaxation, strain-rate and temperature effects. Each one runs entirely in the browser: change the material parameters or the loading, run, and compare the models.

The content is based on the course material of two MSc courses, 1. Mechanics of Materials and 2. Kinetic Processes and Mechanical Behavior in Materials Science, taught in the MSc AM2S (Advanced Manufacturing and Materials Science) at Arts et Métiers Science and Technology.

Nonlinear kinematic hardening

A

Armstrong–Frederick model (1966)

One back stress with hardening and recall terms, plus Voce isotropic hardening. Monotonic test, strain cycles and stress cycles with the analytic ratcheting rate.

 B

Superposition of back stresses (Chaboche, 1986)

Up to four Armstrong–Frederick terms. See which term describes which part of the curve and how each back stress evolves.

 C

Threshold model (Chaboche, 1991)

No recall below a threshold on the last term. Compared with Chaboche for ratcheting and mean stress relaxation.

 D

Static recovery (time-dependent)

The back stress decays with time. Stress relaxation during holds, softening after a rest, and cycles with dwell.

Models designed for ratcheting

E1

Burlet–Cailletaud (1986): radial evanescence

Only the back stress component along the flow is recalled. Tension–torsion ratcheting test compared with Armstrong–Frederick, with the yield surface and plastic strain path.

 E2

Delobelle (1995)

A weighted mix of the two recall rules, tuned by δ′. Multiaxial ratcheting between the two limits.

 E3

Bari–Hassan (2000, 2002)

Three Chaboche terms with Delobelle recall and an optional threshold. Uniaxial and multiaxial ratcheting.

 E4

Ohno–Wang (1993), models I and II

Recall only near a critical state. Chaboche, Ohno–Wang I and II compared for uniaxial and multiaxial ratcheting and mean stress relaxation.

 E5

Abdel-Karim–Ohno (2000)

Ohno–Wang I plus a weak permanent recall μ. Between Ohno–Wang I (μ = 0) and Chaboche (μ = 1): steady, small ratcheting.

 E6

Jiang–Sehitoglu (1996)

Recall growing as a power χ of the back stress, without bracket. Compared with Chaboche and Ohno–Wang II.

 E7

Chen–Jiao–Kim (2005)

Ohno–Wang II with an exponent χ on the bracket: identical in uniaxial loading, more recall under non-proportional loading.

Multisurface and two-surface models

M1

Mróz multisurface model (1967)

Nested yield surfaces pushed by the stress in uniaxial loading: Masing behaviour, memory after inner loops, no ratcheting.

 M2

Iwan (1967) and Besseling (1958) overlay model

Elastic–perfectly plastic elements in parallel. Elements yielding one by one, exact Masing loops and memory, no ratcheting.

 M3

Dafalias–Popov (1975) and Krieg (1975) two-surface model

Plastic modulus set by the distance to a bounding surface. Smooth transitions, the overshoot after partial unloading, ratcheting.

 M4

Yoshida–Uemori model (2002)

Two surfaces and a non-hardening region. Transient Bauschinger effect, permanent softening, workhardening stagnation and modulus decrease.

 M5

Hashiguchi subloading surface model (1989)

No elastic domain: plastic strain grows smoothly with the normal-yield ratio R. Ratcheting below the yield stress, compared with Armstrong–Frederick.

Microstructure-based and distortional models

D1

Teodosiu–Hu (1995)

Dislocation structures and their polarity. Workhardening stagnation after a reversal and cross hardening after an orthogonal path change.

 D2

Barlat HAH, homogeneous anisotropic hardening (2011)

No back stress: the yield surface flattens on the reverse side. Bauschinger effect, transient and permanent softening from distortion alone.

 D3

Feigenbaum–Dafalias (2007): distortional kinematic hardening

An egg-shaped yield surface, sharp in front and flat at the back, linked to the back stress. Watch it turn round under reversed loading.

Elastoviscoplastic (rate-dependent) models

EVP1

Norton power law (1929)

Plastic strain rate as a power of the stress, no elastic domain. Steady creep, relaxation and rate effect, compared with the closed-form solutions.

 EVP2

Bingham (1922) and Perzyna (1966) overstress models

Viscous flow above a yield stress, linear or power law. No creep below the yield stress, relaxation down to it, and the rate-independent limit.

 EVP3

Unified viscoplasticity (Chaboche, 1989)

Armstrong–Frederick back stress, Voce hardening and a Norton flow rule. Loops at two rates, holds, time-dependent ratcheting, primary creep.

 EVP4

Bodner–Partom (1975)

No yield surface: an exponential flow law and a hardening variable driven by plastic work. Smooth yielding, primary creep, cyclic hardening.

 EVP5

Johnson–Cook (1983)

Strain hardening × logarithmic rate term × thermal softening, for high strain rates. Rate sweep up to 10⁴ /s, the cut-off below ε̇0, and relaxation.

Physics-based elastoviscoplastic models

PB-
EVP1

Zerilli–Armstrong (1987)

Dislocation mechanics flow stress, with FCC and BCC forms: temperature and rate act on hardening in copper, on the yield stress in iron.

 PB-
EVP2

Mechanical Threshold Stress (Follansbee–Kocks, 1988)

Thermal activation over a threshold stress that evolves with strain. Rate jumps reveal the effect of the strain-rate history.

 PB-
EVP3

Kocks–Mecking and Estrin–Mecking (1976–1984)

Dislocation density storage and dynamic recovery, Taylor flow stress, grain size. Stage III, rate jumps, primary and steady creep.

 PB-
EVP4

Sellars–Tegart and Garofalo (1963–1966)

Hyperbolic sine law of hot working and creep. Steady flow stress at several temperatures and rates on one Zener–Hollomon master curve.

 PB-
EVP5

Steinberg–Guinan (1980) and Steinberg–Lund (1989)

Shock-loading model: yield stress scaled by the shear modulus, which grows with the pressure and falls to zero at melting. Rate-independent or thermally activated.

 PB-
EVP6

Preston–Tonks–Wallace (2003)

From quasi-static rates to strong shocks: thermal activation, then phonon drag above 10⁷ /s. Rate sweep up to 10⁹ /s.

Other behaviours

Ma

Masing behaviour

Stabilized loops at three amplitudes and their upper branches moved to a common origin. Masing and non-Masing materials.

Simulators A to E7 and D1 to D3 use a thin-walled tube model in tension–torsion; M1 to M5, EVP1 to EVP5 and PB-EVP1 to PB-EVP6 are uniaxial. A to D3 use explicit integration in small increments; the EVP and PB-EVP models integrate in time, implicitly in the plastic strain increment, at constant temperature.
