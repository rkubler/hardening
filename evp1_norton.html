EVP1 · Elastoviscoplastic (rate-dependent) models

Norton power law (1929)

The simplest viscoplastic model: the plastic strain rate is a power of the stress, and there is no elastic domain. Any stress, however small, makes the material flow. Run creep, relaxation, tension at several rates and strain cycles, and compare with the closed-form solutions.

Constitutive equations

ε = εe + εp,   σ = E εe      ε̇p = (|σ| / K)n sign(σ)
  • K (MPa·s1/n) is the drag stress: the stress that gives a plastic strain rate of 1 /s. n is the Norton exponent; it is 3 to 8 for most metals in the power-law creep regime.
  • There is no yield stress and no hardening: the state of the material is described by the stress alone.
  • In multiaxial form, ε̇p = 3⁄2 (J(σ)/K)n s/J(σ), with s the stress deviator.

Closed-form results

  • Creep at constant stress: the strain rate is constant, ε̇ = (σ/K)n. The model only gives secondary (steady-state) creep; primary creep needs hardening (EVP3, EVP4).
  • Tension at constant ε̇: the stress tends to the steady value σ = K ε̇1/n. Multiplying the rate by 100 multiplies the stress by 1001/n; the rate sensitivity d ln σ / d ln ε̇ is 1/n.
  • Relaxation from σ0: σ(t) = [σ01−n + (n − 1) E t / Kn]1/(1−n). The stress decays towards zero, slower and slower: for long times σ ∝ t−1/(n−1), a straight line in log–log axes.

What to look for

Creep. Each curve is a straight line after the loading: steady creep at a rate that grows as σn. The read-out compares the computed rate with (σ/K)n. In the second plot the creep rate is a horizontal line.

Relaxation. The dashed line is the closed-form solution above; the computed curve lies on it. The stress keeps decreasing, with no threshold.

Tension at several rates. No yield point, a smooth curve that saturates at K ε̇1/n, and a straight line of slope 1/n in the second plot (stress against log rate, read on log–log axes: ln σ = ln K + (1/n) ln ε̇).

Strain cycles. At the slow rate the loops are flat and the stresses low; at the fast rate they are higher. There is no Bauschinger effect and no cyclic hardening: the loop is the same from the first cycle on.

Norton's law is mostly used for creep at high temperature, alone or as the flow rule of the unified models (EVP3). Parameters are illustrative.
