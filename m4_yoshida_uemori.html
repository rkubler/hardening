M4 · Multisurface and two-surface models

Yoshida–Uemori model (2002)

A two-surface model built for sheet metal forming and springback. The yield surface (size Y, constant) moves inside a bounding surface that hardens both kinematically and isotropically. It describes the three features of large-strain reverse loading: the transient Bauschinger effect, the permanent softening and the workhardening stagnation. The Young’s modulus can also decrease with plastic strain.

Constitutive equations (uniaxial form)

|σ − α| = Y,   α = β + α*      |σB − β| = B + R
dα* = C [ a ν − √(a/|α*|) α* ] dp,   a = B + R − Y      dβ = m (b ν − β) dp      dR = m (Rsat − R) dp
  • Yield surface of constant size Y, centred on α. ν = ±1 is the direction of plastic flow.
  • Bounding surface of size B + R, centred on β. α* = α − β is the position of the yield surface relative to the bounding surface; it saturates at a = B + R − Y, when the two surfaces touch.
  • C controls how fast the yield surface reaches the bounding surface, so it sets the transient Bauschinger effect (the rounded reverse curve).
  • b, m: the bounding surface centre β saturates at ±b at the rate m. After reversal β has to travel from +b to −b, and meanwhile R hardly grows (see below): the reverse curve stays below the monotonic one, which is the permanent softening.
  • Rsat, m: isotropic hardening of the bounding surface, applied only when β is outside the non-hardening region.
  • Non-hardening region (in β space): an interval [q − r, q + r]. When β moves inside it, R does not grow: this is the workhardening stagnation seen in cyclic tests and after reversal. When β leaves it, the region expands (r) and translates (q); h (between 0 and 1) sets the share of expansion.
  • Young’s modulus: E = E0 − (E0 − Ea)(1 − e−ξ p), important for springback. Set ξ = 0 to keep E constant.

What to look for

Forward–reverse test. The second plot shows the reverse stress (sign changed) against the accumulated strain, next to the monotonic curve. Just after reversal the curve is rounded and much lower (transient Bauschinger effect, set by C). It then becomes parallel to the monotonic curve but stays below it (permanent softening, set by b). For a while the reverse curve is flat or nearly so: β crosses the non-hardening region and R does not grow (stagnation).

Strain cycles. The peak stress grows during the first cycles and then stabilises at a value far below the monotonic curve at the same accumulated strain: once the non-hardening region covers the range of β, cyclic hardening stops. A larger strain amplitude restarts it.

This is a uniaxial version. In the original multiaxial model the non-hardening region is a surface in β space, g(β) = |β − q| − r ≤ 0, whose expansion and translation follow the same rule with h; the uniaxial interval used here is its one-dimensional section. Parameters are illustrative, of the order of those reported for mild and high-strength steel sheets.
